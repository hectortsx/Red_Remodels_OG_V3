# Organic SEO Audit and Action Plan

**Site:** [redremodels.com](https://www.redremodels.com/)
**Reviewed:** October 22, 2026
**Purpose:** Record evidence-based opportunities to grow qualified organic traffic; this is an action backlog, not an implementation.

## Implementation status

The current working branch now permanently redirects `/our-services` and `/our-services/` to the homepage services section, removes the legacy URL from the sitemap, corrects misleading stock-image descriptions and Open Graph/business image attribution, records actual article image dimensions, keeps blog-page structured data page-specific, adds a custom-home-building service page and internal links (the business confirmed this is an active offering), and records the owner-confirmed review date for both cost guides. These changes are not live until deployed. Real project case studies, verification of tenure/schedule/budget claims, and search/analytics data remain outstanding.

## Executive summary

The site has a solid technical foundation for a small static website: the main production pages have unique titles and descriptions, canonicals, a single H1, Open Graph image metadata, and crawlable HTML. `robots.txt` permits crawling, and a sitemap is published.

The clearest near-term issue was that the sitemap listed `/our-services`, a live, self-canonical legacy page with overlapping service content; the working branch now removes it from the sitemap and redirects it to the homepage services section. The next growth opportunity is to strengthen first-hand evidence and trust on service content, then use Search Console and conversion data to choose and measure topics. This review did not have access to Search Console, Google Analytics reports, Google Business Profile performance, backlink data, or PageSpeed/Core Web Vitals field data, so it cannot establish current rankings, traffic levels, or technical performance.

## What is already working

- Main production pages are static, server-rendered HTML. The homepage, contact page, and nine service pages each have a title, meta description, one canonical, one H1, and Open Graph image metadata.
- Service pages have descriptive Denver-oriented titles and useful buyer information, including scope, process, cost guidance, and calls to action.
- `robots.txt` allows crawling and names the sitemap. The sitemap contains the homepage, contact page, blog, and all nine current service pages.
- The homepage and contact page include business/contact structured data; the homepage identifies service areas and social profiles.
- Blog posts link to relevant service/contact pages, and the blog build script generates `BlogPosting` structured data.

These checks are not a guarantee of indexation, rich-result eligibility, rankings, or page-speed quality.

## Findings and recommended actions

### P1 — Redirect the legacy `/our-services` URL and keep it out of the sitemap

**Evidence:** At review time, the live `/our-services` route returned HTTP 200, had a self-referencing canonical, and served the older services page. It was also included in the sitemap alongside the current homepage and dedicated service pages.

**Why it matters:** Search engines can treat this as a separate canonical page even though its service content overlaps the current site. It may split relevance and links, surface older positioning, and send visitors to a less useful page.

**Action:** The working branch retires the legacy route with a permanent redirect to the homepage services section and removes it from the sitemap. Before deployment, review any inbound links and test the CloudFront function in the intended distribution configuration. Do not hand-edit generated legacy Duda output.

**References:** [sitemap.xml](../public/sitemap.xml), [CloudFront URL rewrite](../infra/cloudfront-functions/url-rewrite.js), [legacy services page](../public/pages/desktop/our-services/index.html)

### P1 — Add first-hand project proof to the highest-value service pages

**Evidence:** The service pages explain services and costs, but the current site mostly presents generic service descriptions and stock-photo assets rather than identifiable completed Red Remodels projects. The homepage makes claims such as “20+ years,” “on time and on budget,” and locally owned; service pages also give detailed cost ranges and process claims.

**Why it matters:** Remodeling decisions are high-consideration and local. Original, verifiable evidence helps prospective customers assess fit and gives the site more distinctive, experience-based content than generic descriptions alone.

**Action:** Build a small portfolio of actual projects and add relevant examples to the matching service pages: Denver-area location at an appropriate privacy level, project scope, the homeowner’s problem, materials or constraints, timeline where supportable, and original before/after photos with accurate captions and alt text. Link each example to its service page and contact CTA. Substantiate experience, licensing/insurance, warranties, and schedule/budget claims where applicable; omit claims that cannot be verified. Do not publish customer details or images without permission.

**References:** [homepage](../public/index.html), [service pages](../public/services)

### P2 — Add first-hand custom-home evidence when approved materials are available

**Evidence:** The business confirmed custom-home construction is an active service and a dedicated landing page is now linked from the homepage and service navigation. No approved project examples or photos were provided.

**Why it matters:** Real examples can demonstrate experience and make the new service page more distinctive. Claims about completed work should be supportable.

**Action:** The unverified time-lapse embed and specific build claims have been removed from the article. Add approved project details and photos when available; do not imply stock imagery depicts Red Remodels work.

**Reference:** [custom-home post source](../blog/posts/what-are-the-steps-to-build-a-custom-home.md)

### P2 — Make cost guides demonstrably local, current, and trustworthy

**Evidence:** The kitchen and bathroom guides contain Denver-area prices, labor shares, material ranges, and market assertions. The business confirmed the published cost estimates are approved and current; a named expert reviewer and citations for external market assertions are not available. The bathroom guide is dated April 30, 2026, and the kitchen guide is dated March 13, 2026.

**Why it matters:** Cost content can attract high-intent searches, but precise estimates need context and credibility. Unsourced claims can become stale or mislead readers as labor and materials change.

**Action:** The approved cost figures now display an October 22, 2026 review date in the post and structured metadata. Keep estimates scoped (project size, labor/material assumptions, location, exclusions, and factors that change cost); cite reputable sources for external market claims or identify company estimates as such. Add a named reviewer only when a real qualified reviewer is identified. Cross-link each guide to its matching service page and, when available, real project examples.

**References:** [bathroom cost guide](../blog/posts/bathroom-remodel-cost-denver-2026.md), [kitchen cost guide](../blog/posts/how-much-does-a-kitchen-remodel-cost-in-denver.md)

### P2 — Use accurate image attribution and descriptions

**Evidence:** Before this action, the kitchen cost guide used the generic Red Remodels Open Graph graphic as its article image, while its `imageAlt` said it was a kitchen remodel completed by the company. Several service pages used a generic brand graphic with alt text claiming it showed Red Remodels work; the homepage business schema and social card also referenced a stock kitchen photo as the company image.

**Why it matters:** Inaccurate image descriptions and implied project attribution can mislead readers and search engines, and reduce trust. Generic or unrelated images also miss the chance to show authentic work in image results and social previews.

**Action:** The kitchen article now uses a relevant kitchen image; stock-photo descriptions no longer attribute the pictured work to Red Remodels. Service Open Graph descriptions now match the brand graphic, and the homepage social/schema image points to that same branded asset. Use original project images only when they truly depict the described work and permission is in place.

**References:** [kitchen cost guide](../blog/posts/how-much-does-a-kitchen-remodel-cost-in-denver.md), [custom-home post](../blog/posts/what-are-the-steps-to-build-a-custom-home.md), [blog generator](../scripts/build-blog.js)

### P2 — Build topic coverage around services and connect the content

**Evidence:** There are currently three blog sources: two cost guides and one custom-home guide. The site has nine service lines. Blog posts do link to relevant services, but most service areas do not yet have supporting educational content or visible project examples.

**Why it matters:** Focused, useful coverage can help the site serve customers at research and comparison stages, while internal links help readers and crawlers discover the relevant service pages. Publishing more pages alone is not a goal; each page should answer a distinct customer question with reliable, local detail.

**Action:** Use Search Console queries and customer questions to select a few topics for the most commercially relevant services. Prioritize useful comparisons, planning/permit considerations, realistic cost drivers, maintenance, and documented project case studies. Give every article a clear primary service-page destination, link both ways where useful, and avoid thin neighborhood pages or repetitive keyword-targeted posts. Add service-area references only where the company genuinely works and can provide local detail.

**References:** [blog sources](../blog/posts), [service pages](../public/services), [blog generator](../scripts/build-blog.js)

### P3 — Keep the sitemap accurate and base updates on real edits

**Evidence:** The sitemap's homepage and contact-page `lastmod` values were November 1, 2025, while service and blog entries had later dates. The blog build script replaces blog entries but retains the existing static-page entries.

**Why it matters:** The sitemap should describe the preferred, current URLs. Search engines may use `lastmod` when it is trustworthy; dates that do not reflect meaningful page changes are not useful.

**Action:** The legacy URL has been removed, the new custom-home-building page added, and the homepage/service-page modification dates updated to reflect their navigation/content changes. Check that each sitemap URL returns the preferred page and matches its canonical. Update static `lastmod` only when the corresponding page has materially changed; do not change dates simply to make them look recent. `changefreq` and `priority` are optional hints and should not take precedence over accurate URLs and dates.

**References:** [sitemap.xml](../public/sitemap.xml), [blog generator](../scripts/build-blog.js)

### P3 — Establish a performance and organic-conversion baseline

**Evidence:** This review did not inspect Search Console, Analytics reports, Business Profile performance, backlink data, or field performance data. The site includes a Google Analytics tag in its source, but source presence does not confirm correct event tracking or meaningful reporting.

**Action:** Before judging impact, record a baseline for at least the prior 3–6 months:

1. In Search Console, review clicks, impressions, CTR, and average position by query and landing page; inspect indexing/canonical reports and sitemap processing.
2. In Analytics, verify organic landing-page sessions and track qualified form submissions and phone-call actions as conversions.
3. In Google Business Profile, review calls, website visits, and direction requests; confirm business name, phone, service area, hours, categories, and services are accurate and consistent. For a service-area business, do not publish a private address unless it is a genuine customer-facing location.
4. Measure mobile Core Web Vitals and page performance with field data where available, and prioritize any demonstrated issue on the homepage, service pages, and blog templates.

Use this baseline to choose the first service/topic improvements and compare results after the work is indexed. Avoid promising a traffic lift before this data exists.

## Suggested sequence

1. **Before deployment:** Test the CloudFront redirect and confirm the sitemap/canonical URL set.
2. **Next:** Add approved project details and photos to the most commercially important service pages.
3. **In parallel:** Capture the Search Console, Analytics, Business Profile, and mobile performance baseline.
4. **Ongoing:** Publish only evidence-backed, locally useful content selected from real search/customer data; review results quarterly.

## Review scope and limitations

Reviewed the live homepage, robots file, sitemap, selected service/blog/contact routes, and repository source for the homepage, contact page, service pages, blog sources, blog generator, sitemap, and CloudFront URL rewrite. The live URL checks confirmed HTTP 200 for `/our-services` and `/our-services/`, `/contact-us`, and `/services/kitchen-remodels` and `/services/kitchen-remodels/`; the service page canonicals use the slashless URL form.

This was a source-and-page review, not a full crawler or technical SEO certification. It did not verify Search Console index status, analytics data quality, redirects for every URL, backlink profile, local pack rankings, structured-data eligibility in Google’s validators, image licensing, accessibility beyond basic source signals, or real-user Core Web Vitals. Validate those items during implementation.
