What Are the Steps to Build a Custom Home in Colorado?

By Red Remodels  ·  March 20, 2026

Updated October 22, 2026.

Building a custom home involves a series of decisions about the property, design, materials, schedule, and budget. Understanding the main stages can help you plan questions to discuss with a builder before work begins.

Red Remodels builds custom homes in the Denver area. Each project is different, so the details, responsibilities, and schedule depend on the property and agreed scope. Here's a general overview of the process.

The Custom Home Building Process, Step by Step

1. Site Preparation and Foundation Work

Every home starts with the property and foundation. Site conditions, grading, drainage, and local requirements should be reviewed as part of planning, with foundation design determined by the project team and qualified professionals.

Get this wrong and no amount of beautiful finishes will save you. Get it right and you've built something meant to last generations.

2. Framing — Where Your Home Takes Shape

Framing establishes the structure and room layout. Structural plans guide the work, and rough electrical, plumbing, and HVAC are coordinated as the build progresses.

3. Exterior Finishes and Curb Appeal

Exterior materials and windows affect appearance, durability, and project cost. Selections should take the property's conditions and the owner's priorities into account, and should be coordinated early enough to support the project schedule.

4. Interior Build-Out and Systems

Once the structure is enclosed, interior work may include insulation, drywall, flooring, cabinetry, tile, and paint. Selections and trade scheduling should be coordinated with the agreed scope.

Don't Forget the Landscape — Plan It Early

Landscaping is easier to consider before the build is complete. Grading, drainage, irrigation, and hardscape can affect how outdoor areas connect with the house, so include them in early planning discussions.

Plan the Schedule with the Project Team

Custom-home schedules vary with design decisions, site conditions, approvals, material availability, weather, and the agreed scope. Ask how timing will be estimated, what assumptions it depends on, and how changes will be handled.
